Getting Started

  1. 1 Open Song Buddy. Each song you work on lives in its own .songbuddy file — tap New Document to create one, or open an existing one from Files.
  2. 2 You'll see two options: Separate Audio… to split a song into its individual parts (stems), or Add Track… to bring in an audio file as-is. Both accept audio or video files — for a video, Song Buddy pulls out the soundtrack automatically.
  3. 3 On Mac, you can also drag and drop an audio file directly onto the window.
  4. 4 Once your stems are ready, the waveforms for each part appear as coloured rows. Tap or click anywhere on a waveform to jump to that point in the song.
💡 Song Buddy supports any audio format your device can read — MP3, AAC, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, and more. You can also drop in a video file (MP4, MOV, and more) and Song Buddy will extract its soundtrack for you.
✂️

Separating a Song into Stems

  1. 1 Tap Separate Audio… and pick an audio or video file from your device. If you choose a video, Song Buddy extracts its soundtrack first.
  2. 2 Song Buddy will separate the song into up to 6 stems: vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano, and other. The whole process runs on your device — no internet required.
  3. 3 A progress bar shows how far along the separation is. Most songs take a few minutes. You can cancel at any time.
  4. 4 When separation is complete, all stems appear as coloured waveform rows ready to play.
  5. 5 For cleaner stems, set Separation quality to High — in Settings → Audio on Mac, or in the song's Settings on iPhone/iPad. High averages several passes, so it takes several times longer.
💡 Separation quality depends on the source recording. Clean studio recordings tend to separate more cleanly than live recordings with heavy reverb or distortion.
📱 On iPhone and iPad, separating needs an A14 chip or newer (iPhone 12 and later). On older devices the Separate button is turned off, but you can still open and practise with songs separated by Song Buddy on your Mac.
📥

Opening & Importing from Other Apps

  1. 1 You don't have to start inside Song Buddy. On iPhone/iPad, find an audio or video file in the Files app, tap Share → Song Buddy (or use "Open in"), and it hands the file straight over.
  2. 2 You can also share a video straight from the Photos app, or from any app with a share sheet — pick Song Buddy from the row of apps.
  3. 3 Song Buddy asks to confirm, then separates the file into stems and saves it as a new .songbuddy song in your Song Buddy folder, ready to open and play.
  4. 4 On Mac, right-click an audio or video file in Finder and choose Open With → Song Buddy, or drop it onto the Song Buddy Dock icon. A fresh window opens and separation starts right away — your other open songs are never touched.
💡 Importing from another app always creates a brand-new song, so it will never overwrite the project you already have open.

Playback Controls

  1. 1 Tap Play / Pause to start or stop playback. The return-to-start button jumps back to the beginning.
  2. 2 Mute a stem by tapping the speaker icon on its row — the waveform dims to show it's silent.
  3. 3 Solo a stem to hear only that part. All other stems are silenced while a stem is soloed. Tap solo again to release it.
  4. 4 Drag the volume slider on each stem row to balance the mix exactly how you want it.
  5. 5 The master volume sets the overall output level. It's at the top of the Practice panel on Mac and iPad, and in the song's Settings on iPhone.
  6. 6 Tap or click anywhere on a waveform to seek to that position. On Mac, you can also drag the playhead directly.
  7. 7 Give a stem your own colour: tap the colour well beside its name and pick any shade. The choice is saved with the song, so your colour scheme is there every time you reopen it.
💡 To practice a bass line, solo the bass stem. To play along on guitar, mute the guitar stem and leave everything else audible.
🔁

Loop Regions

  1. 1 On Mac, hold Shift and drag across any waveform to paint a loop region. The highlighted area will repeat continuously.
  2. 2 On iPhone/iPad, open the Regions sheet and tap + to create a loop at the current playhead position. You can then drag its edges on the waveform to adjust it.
  3. 3 You can save multiple named loops in one document — perfect for marking the verse, chorus, bridge, or a tricky solo.
  4. 4 Tap a loop in the Regions list to activate it. The loop toggle in the transport controls must be enabled for the region to repeat. The active loop's name shows on the bar above the waveforms. On Mac, press ← / → to move to the previous or next loop, or ⌘1–9 to jump straight to one.
  5. 5 Drag a loop's edges to resize it — playback carries on without a gap. On iPhone/iPad, turn on Snap region selection to beat in the song's Settings to make the edges land on the beat.
  6. 6 Rename a loop by double-clicking its name on Mac, or by tapping and holding the row on iOS.
  7. 7 Delete a loop with the trash icon (Mac) or a swipe-to-delete gesture (iOS).
💡 Set a tight loop around just two or four bars of a difficult passage. Once you can play it cleanly at full speed, widen the loop to include more context.

Speed & Pitch

  1. 1 Speed, pitch, zoom, master volume and the metronome live in the Practice panel, with large controls that are easy to reach while you hold your instrument. On Mac, click the panel button at the right of the toolbar (or press ⌃⌘I). On iPad, tap Settings (gear icon) at the top right. On iPhone, tap the gear icon in the bottom toolbar.
  2. 2 Speed sets the playback rate from 30% to 100%, and the pitch stays the same. Drag the slider, use the − / + buttons (hold them to keep going), or tap a preset: 50%, 75%, 90%, 100%.
  3. 3 Start slow — around 50–75% — until you can play the passage cleanly, then raise the speed step by step until you reach 100%.
  4. 4 Pitch shifts the whole song up or down in semitones (±12 st) without changing speed. Use it to match a song to your instrument's tuning, or to move it to an easier key.
  5. 5 Zoom sets how much of the song fits on screen. You can also pinch on the waveforms, or press ⌘+ / ⌘− on Mac.
  6. 6 Each control has a Reset button that takes it back to 100%, 0 st, or the whole song. Speed and pitch are saved with your .songbuddy file, so they're restored next time you open it.
🥁

Metronome

  1. 1 The metronome lives in the Practice panel, below speed and pitch (see Speed & Pitch for how to open it). On iPhone, it also has its own button in the bottom toolbar. On Mac, press M to turn it on or off.
  2. 2 Toggle Enable to turn on the click track. Song Buddy automatically detects the tempo — no manual BPM entry needed. The tempo readout follows your playback speed, so slowing the song down slows the click to match.
  3. 3 By default the beat is detected from the most rhythmic part (usually the drums). To use a different stem, open that stem's menu — right-click its row on Mac, or tap its name on iPhone/iPad — and choose Use for Beat Detection.
  4. 4 If the detected tempo seems doubled or halved, use the ÷2 or ×2 buttons to correct it, or tap re-detect to analyse the beat afresh.
  5. 5 When the beat lands somewhere ÷2/×2 can't fix, use Tap Tempo: tap the button in time with the music and Song Buddy rebuilds the grid from your taps. Tapping while the song plays locks in both the tempo and where the beats fall.
  6. 6 The volume slider controls how loud the click is in the mix.
💡 The metronome follows the drummer's natural timing where beats were detected, then fills in the gaps on the grid — so it stays tight even through tempo variations.

Bass Notes

  1. 1 Song Buddy can read the bass line for you and show every note on a scrolling piano-roll lane beneath the waveforms — ideal for learning a line note for note.
  2. 2 Open the bass stem's menu — right-click its row on Mac, or tap its name on iPhone/iPad — and choose Detect Notes. Detection runs on your device; a progress bar appears with a Stop button if you want to cancel.
  3. 3 When it finishes, the piano-roll lane appears. Each note is drawn at its pitch and length, and the lane scrolls in time as the song plays so you can follow along. The note that's playing is outlined.
  4. 4 Use the Previous Note and Next Note buttons (⌥← / ⌥→ on Mac) to step through the line one note at a time and hear each one.
  5. 5 The detected notes are saved with your .songbuddy file, so they're there the next time you open it — no need to re-detect.
  6. 6 To remove them, open the bass stem's menu again and choose Clear Notes.
💡 Pair the piano-roll with a tight loop and reduced speed: solo the bass, loop a couple of bars, slow it to 60%, and read the notes off the lane until they're under your fingers.

Tuner

  1. 1 The chromatic tuner lives inside the Input panel. Open the Input panel from the toolbar on Mac, or the Input sheet from the toolbar menu (or the opening screen) on iPhone/iPad, then switch on the Tuner section at the top. A green dot shows it's listening.
  2. 2 Play a note on your instrument into the microphone or audio interface. The tuner shows the nearest note name, octave, and how many cents sharp or flat you are.
  3. 3 The bar turns green when you are within ±8 cents of the target pitch — that's in tune.
  4. 4 Switch the Tuner section off when you're done — it stops listening straight away. Closing the Input panel also stops it.
💡 If the song is pitched-shifted in Song Buddy, tune your instrument to match the shifted pitch before playing along.
🎤

Live Input

  1. 1 Connect your instrument or microphone. On Mac, open the floating Input panel from the toolbar (⌘I). On iPad, tap Input at the top right. On iPhone, tap the Input button in the toolbar — or the Input button on the opening screen, so you can set up your sound before opening any song.
  2. 2 Pick your input device, then turn on Live Input to hear yourself through the effects in real time (⌘⇧I on Mac).
  3. 3 If your audio interface has more than one input, choose the one your instrument is plugged into with Input channel. Song Buddy remembers your choice for each interface. Auto picks it for you.
  4. 4 The built-in amp comes with a compressor, a choice of cabinets, and an EQ & Ambience section with a 3-band EQ and reverb. A 25 Hz high-pass filter removes rumble without cutting your low E.
  5. 5 Save your favourite settings as a preset with the + button (Mac) or Save (iPhone/iPad) in the Input panel. The starred preset is the default for new songs. Presets are shared across all your documents.
💡 Pause the song to hear only your instrument through the effects.
🔌

Audio Plugins

  1. 1 You can play through your own Audio Unit effects — amp simulators, pedals, delays — instead of the built-in amp. On iPhone/iPad, install the plugin's app from the App Store and open it once so it shows up.
  2. 2 In the Input panel, turn on the Audio plugin section and pick a plugin from the menu. Your own plugins are listed under Your plugins, Apple's under Apple. Choose Built-in to go back to Song Buddy's amp.
  3. 3 Turn on Live Input, then tap Edit… to open the plugin's own controls. Plugins without their own screen get a simple list of sliders.
  4. 4 Song Buddy remembers the plugin and its settings, and saves them in your input presets too.
💡 On Mac, some older plugins make macOS ask to lower Song Buddy's security settings. Choose Lower Security Settings to use the plugin; macOS asks again after each Song Buddy update. If a plugin doesn't load, turn Live Input off and on to try again.
📤

Exporting Stems

  1. 1 On Mac, go to File → Export tracks… (⌘E). A save panel opens — choose a folder and Song Buddy copies all stem files there.
  2. 2 On iPhone/iPad, long-press a stem row and tap Export to share the individual stem file via AirDrop, Files, or any other app.
  3. 3 Stems are exported as Apple Lossless (ALAC) m4a files — lossless quality at roughly half the size of WAV.
  4. 4 You can import exported stems into any DAW (Logic Pro, GarageBand, Ableton, etc.) for further editing or mixing.
☁️

iCloud Sync

  1. 1 Song Buddy keeps your songs in a Song Buddy folder in iCloud Drive. Save a song there and it shows up on every device signed into the same Apple Account — separate on your Mac, practise on your iPhone.
  2. 2 The folder appears in the Files app (iPhone/iPad) and in Finder → iCloud Drive (Mac). Songs imported from another app are saved here automatically.
  3. 3 Everything a song needs — stems, loops, tempo, detected notes, and your colours — travels inside the single .songbuddy file, so nothing is left behind when it syncs.
💡 To avoid sync conflicts, let a song finish uploading on one device before opening it on another — a spinning cloud icon next to the file means it's still syncing.
⚙️

Settings

  1. 1 On Mac, open Settings via the Song Buddy menu → Settings… (⌘,). It has three tabs: Audio, Master and General. On iPhone/iPad, open the song's Settings with the gear icon.
  2. 2 Audio (Mac) — Choose the input and output device, the default master volume for new documents, and the separation quality.
  3. 3 Master — A 3-band Master EQ and a Master limiter that apply to all songs. The limiter's pre-gain makes playback louder without clipping. On iPhone/iPad these are in the song's Settings.
  4. 4 General (Mac) — Set a default export folder, bring back the welcome screen, or Reset all settings. Resetting doesn't touch your songs or saved presets.
  5. 5 On Mac, Help → Keyboard Shortcuts lists every shortcut.
